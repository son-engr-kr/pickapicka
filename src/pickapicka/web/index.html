First, choose where to keep your projects

Your photos are never moved, renamed or changed. The app keeps its own work (your picks, edits and thumbnails) in a separate projects folder, with one subfolder per shoot. The app calls this folder a workspace.

Recommended

  • Pick a drive with room to spare: thumbnails and previews are stored here.
  • Keep it apart from your photos. Don't choose a photo folder, or a folder inside one.
  • You can add more projects folders later, for example one per drive.

Choose the folder of one shoot, such as a wedding or a trip. Subfolders inside it are included, and each one can become a scene. RAW files count too; when a shot has both a RAW and a JPEG, the RAW is used. Nothing in the folder is changed.

2. Name the project & group scenes

The project is created inside your current workspace. Just give it a name — no need to pick a folder.

Group scenes

Photos are culled one scene at a time: the suggestions compare each photo with the others in its scene, so a scene should be one moment or setting. You can switch this later from the sidebar without re-scoring.

              Light
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Color
              
          
                
                
              
          
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Tone curve 
              
          
              
          
                
              
          
              
            
          
            
          
              Color Mixer 
              
          
                
          
                  Eight hue bands, blended into each other rather than windowed, so
                  there is no seam between orange and yellow. Near-greys are left
                  alone: their hue is numerical noise, and pushing it would speckle
                  a flat wall.
                
          
                
          
                
          
                
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Color Grading 
              
          
                
          
                  A hue and a strength per tonal region. Adding colour never moves
                  brightness: every tint is a vector with zero luma, so the
                  exposure you set stays the exposure you get.
                
          
                
          
                
          
                
          
                
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Detail & Effects
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Creative
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Look 
              
          
                
          
                  A colour lookup table under every slider, the way a profile sits
                  under Lightroom's: import a .cube, or match this
                  photo's colours to another frame of the same scene, such as one
                  from a second camera. Whole photo only.
                
          
                
          
                  
                  
                
          
                
                
          
                  
                  
                
          
                
                
          
                  
          
                    Pick the frame to match. It is taken as you edited it; this photo
                    is taken as shot, and your sliders here then apply on top. Works
                    best on the same subject under the same light.
                  
          
                  
          
                
          
                
          
              
          
            
          
            
          
              Film 
              
          
                
                
          
                  A response curve applied to log exposure, a dye-crosstalk matrix
                  in density space, highlight scatter that bleeds warm, and grain
                  whose size is fixed against the frame rather than the render.
                
          
                
          
                
          
              
          
            
          
          
          
          
          
            
          
              Heal & red eye 
              
          
                
          
                  
                  
                  
                  
                  
                
          
                
                
                
          
                  Heal fills in from the edges: right for dust, spots, a stray hair
                  or a wire against the sky, but it smears texture like skin,
                  fabric, grass or hair. AI fill draws that texture instead (a
                  28 MB MIT-licensed model), and Generative redraws larger
                  regions with a diffusion model (1.9 GB, opt-in); Clone copies
                  real texture from a place you choose. Runs before every other
                  adjustment.

                  a group photo get the same look. A face can have settings of its own.

          Re-score photos
          
        
      
          
      
          
        
          
          Scoring measures sharpness and exposure and detects faces. Pick what
          this project is a shoot of and it detects that too: photos
          without one sort down, sharpness is measured on the subject instead of
          the whole frame, and look-alike subjects get grouped.
        
          
        
        
          
        
        
          
        
          
        
          
          Decisions and edits are preserved. Grouping runs straight afterwards,
          because scoring rebuilds the face embeddings the groups are built from.
